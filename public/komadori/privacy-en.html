Komadori Privacy Policy

This applies to the app “Komadori” (コマドリ). The iPhone and Android versions send and do not send the same things. 日本語
This is the privacy policy of the app “Komadori” (iPhone and Android). The upload feature of the website (shogi.nkkuma.tokyo) is handled differently; see the website privacy policy and terms (Japanese). This English page is a translation of the Japanese policy. If the two differ, the Japanese version prevails.

Komadori is an app that makes game records (kifu) from shogi games. It can be used in two ways: you can import a video you filmed earlier, or the app can record the game with the camera while you play. In the app’s settings you choose how games are recorded: Ume or Take (Take is the default; every time before Take sends a recording, the app shows on screen what is sent and what is kept). Earlier versions also offered Matsu. With Ume, the video of the game is processed only on your device. With Take and Matsu, the recording itself is sent to our server and kept there. That is why this page explains specifically what leaves your device, what does not, and what happens to what leaves.

How the camera is used

The camera is used only when you record a game. It is not used when you import a video.

  • The only permission we ask for is the camera. The device asks for camera permission the first time you open game recording. Without it, game recording can’t be used, but importing videos still works.
  • We do not ask for access to your photos and videos. Through the device’s photo picker, the app receives only the one video you choose. The app never browses the photos and videos on your device.
  • No sound is recorded. Game recording captures camera images only. The microphone is not used, in Ume, Take or Matsu. Conversations and sounds during the game are not kept on the device or on the server.

Information we collect

To build a game record from a video, the app sends the following to our server.

  • Still images to locate the board (up to 3): at the start of conversion, up to 3 still images of the whole picture are sent to find where the board is. Depending on how you film, people or the surroundings may appear. These images are not stored on the server and do not remain after the board has been located.
  • Cut-out board images: still images of only the board area, cut from the video at the moment of each move. A short game is about 1–2 MB in total; even a long game is about 20 MB.
  • Move detection data (numbers): numbers that the app computed on the device describing which squares changed and when, plus numbers such as the video length, screen size and board position. They are used to build the game record and are neither images nor text. The video file name is replaced with a random name on import, so the original file name is not sent.
  • Position text: when you replay a record on the board, a short text describing the arrangement of the pieces (SFEN) is sent to show the evaluation and a suggested next move. It contains no images or video. Positions sent are not stored.
  • Technical logs of communication: when the app connects to the server, access records such as the IP address and time are kept for technical reasons. They are used only to prevent unauthorized access and to deal with failures, and are deleted automatically after one month.

While recording a game with Ume (on this device), the only thing sent to the server is the still images to locate the board (whole screen, up to 3, not stored on the server). Moves are read on the device. If you later read a game recorded with Ume again from the recording, what is sent is the same as when importing a video: the still images to locate the board (up to 3) and the board images cut out at each move. The recording itself is not sent. How they are handled afterwards is described under “Handling on the server and retention” below.

Take and Matsu: modes that send the recording itself

When Take is selected under “How to record games” in Settings (or Matsu in earlier versions), the recording of the game itself is sent to the server. This is handled differently from the uses described above. Before starting, the app shows on screen what is sent and what is kept. Nothing starts until you press the button.

What is sent depends on the mode you choose:

  • Take (send while recording): during the game, moves are read on the device as with Ume and shown as a draft record. The recording is sent little by little during the game, and after you stop, the server reads the whole game again to make the final record. The only other thing sent is the still images to locate the board (up to 3). Both the recording and the still images show the whole screen. They are not cut down to the board, so your opponent and people nearby appear.
  • Matsu (video-quality live, earlier versions only): can’t be selected in the current version. When it was selected in earlier versions, the video was cut into clips of a few seconds during the game, and joined into one on the server after you stopped.

No sound is recorded and the microphone is not used, in every mode. Conversations and sounds during the game are not kept on the device or on the server.

What is sent when showing the evaluation

When you open a record in “Replay on board”, only a short text describing the arrangement of the pieces in that position (SFEN) is sent to the evaluation server (AWS Tokyo region), to show who is ahead and a suggested next move. The number of candidate moves and a number setting the thinking time are sent with it. No video or images are sent. Positions sent are used only for the calculation and are not stored. The arrangement of the pieces contains no names of the players and nothing that identifies the device. This is the same for games recorded with Ume, Take or Matsu, and for games made from a video.

What never leaves your device

  • Sound never leaves your device in any use. It is not recorded in the first place.
  • The video itself is not sent when you record with Ume (on this device) or when you make a record from a video you filmed. The video of the game stays only on your device. The recording itself is sent only when you choose Take or Matsu as described above.
  • We never ask you to enter personal information such as your name or contact details. There is no account registration.

Video kept on your device

The video of a recorded game stays on your device for a while, so that you can check later whether a move was read correctly. The app never sends this video anywhere by itself. Video is sent to the server only when you choose Take or Matsu above and record on the spot.

  • You choose how many days to keep it in the app’s settings, from three options. With the shortest option, the video is deleted as soon as the game ends.
  • You can delete it at any time: for one game with “Delete this game’s video”, or all at once with “Delete all now” in Settings. Deleted video can’t be restored. The game records stay.
  • Besides when the days run out, the oldest games are deleted first when the storage area is full or the device is low on space. Such games then show “The video is no longer available.” Nothing is deleted silently.
  • It does not go into the Photos app or gallery, or into automatic backups such as iCloud. The only way to hand it over is the iPhone version’s “Share video (AirDrop, etc.)” button. The Android version has no way to hand over video.
  • If you use a filming consent form at a venue, that written promise comes first. If the form sets a date by which video must be deleted, shorten the number of days in Settings so that it is not exceeded.

The Android version

What is sent and not sent is exactly as described above. In addition, the Android version works as follows.

  • It does not ask for permission to access photos and videos. Through the device’s photo picker, it receives only the one video you choose. The app never browses the photos and videos on your device. The app declares only two permissions: internet access (INTERNET) to communicate with the server, and the camera (CAMERA) for game recording.
  • The app keeps one copy of the video you chose, so that you can look back at the board at each move after conversion. It is kept in the app’s private storage, not visible to other apps or the device’s gallery. When you convert the next video, the previous copy is deleted. The original video is not changed (not moved or deleted). The video of recorded games is kept separately, as described under “Video kept on your device”.
  • Data on the device is not included in automatic backup. Game records and video copies are not carried automatically to backup destinations outside the device.
  • Game records leave the app only when you export them as KIF and send them from the share menu. You choose who receives them. There is no way to hand video to other apps.

Purposes of use

We use the information we collect only for the following purposes:

  • To make game records from game videos
  • To investigate the cause when conversion fails (only the technical logs described above are used for this; cut-out board images are deleted as soon as conversion ends and are not used; video received with Take and Matsu is not used for this purpose either)
  • To calculate and show the evaluation and a suggested next move when you replay a record on the board
  • To prevent unauthorized access and to respond to inquiries
  • To use video received with Take and Matsu to improve how boards and pieces are read

Handling on the server and retention

Still images to locate the board are used only to find the board and are not stored on the server. Cut-out board images are used only to build the game record and are deleted as soon as processing ends. In case deletion fails, or conversion does not run, data on the server is set to be deleted automatically within one day at most.

The server is in Japan (AWS Tokyo region).

What stays on the server with Take and Matsu

Only when Take or Matsu is chosen, the following stays on the server. It is not deleted after the game record is made.

  • Video with the sound removed: the recording you sent, with the audio removed. It shows the game as it happened.
  • Still images sent during the game: whole-screen still images (sent every few seconds by Take in earlier versions; not sent in the current version).
  • Video clips sent during the game: clips a few seconds long (with Matsu).
  • Images of the board straightened to a front view, and the finished game record.

Why we keep it: to improve how boards and pieces are read. Komadori reads more accurately the more it learns from videos of real games. Kept video is used only for that learning.

How long we keep it: there is no fixed period. We delete it when we decide it is no longer used for that learning. If you ask us to delete it, we delete it regardless of the period (see “Requests for deletion and access”).

Who sees it: only the operator (Akira Sato). The storage cannot be accessed directly from outside, and communication is encrypted (HTTPS). It is not published or given to third parties. No data is sent to advertising or analytics companies.

No sound is kept, because none is recorded.

Disclosure to third parties

We do not give the information we collect to third parties (except where required by law). No data is sent to advertising or analytics companies.

We use Amazon Web Services (AWS) as our server infrastructure. AWS does not use the content of the images.

The iPhone version has a way (such as AirDrop) to hand the video of a game to someone in front of you on the spot. You choose who receives it. We cannot know how the video is handled after it is handed over. If your opponent or people nearby appear in it, please ask them before handing it over. The Android version has no way to hand over video.

Security

Communication is encrypted (HTTPS), and storage cannot be accessed directly from outside. Most exchanges with the server are restricted with authentication.

For users in the EEA, Switzerland and the UK

  • Controller: Akira Sato (contact below).
  • Legal bases: making a game record and showing the evaluation are done because you ask the app to do them (performance of the service you request). Keeping video sent with Take to improve reading is based on your consent, which you give when you choose Take and start recording; you can withdraw it at any time by switching to Ume and by asking us to delete video already sent. Technical logs are kept for our legitimate interest in keeping the service secure.
  • Transfer outside your country: the server is in Japan (AWS Tokyo region). Japan is covered by an adequacy decision of the European Commission.
  • Your rights: you can ask for access to, correction or deletion of your data, ask us to restrict or stop processing it, and withdraw consent, by writing to the contact below. You can also lodge a complaint with the data protection authority in your country.
  • Children: do not choose Take for games of children without the consent of a parent or guardian (see “When filming” below).

Requests for deletion and access

If you want kept video deleted, please contact us at the address below. We will look for it and delete it. We won’t ask why, and there is no fee. We will reply within 14 days of your message. You may write in English or Japanese.

Nothing we keep identifies whose it is (no account, no name, no number that identifies the device). So please tell us some clues to find it:

  • The date and approximate time of filming
  • The mode you had chosen (Take or Matsu)
  • The approximate length of the game

What we find from the clues is deleted, and we reply that it has been deleted. If the clues are not enough to find it, we will tell you so. We will never answer “deleted” without finding it. Either way, we reply within 14 days.

Please contact us in the same way if you were not filming yourself but appeared in the video because you were there. Whether the request comes from the person who filmed or from someone who appeared, we delete it if the clues match.

Data deleted after processing, such as cut-out board images, no longer exists at the time of your request. In that case we will explain so.

Questions and requests about how data is handled are accepted at the contact below. You can also reach the same address from the app’s Settings.

When filming

The request is the same whether you film a video or record a game. The camera can be straight above the board, or beside the board looking down a little. Avoid a low position where the board looks squashed. In any direction, choose a place where faces are not in the picture, and check on screen before you start. Even so, people or the surroundings may appear depending on how you film. When filming where your opponent or others appear, please ask them first. When filming children, get the consent of a parent or guardian. Recorded video stays on your device, so for children’s games we recommend shortening the number of days kept or deleting it after the game. With Take and Matsu, the video stays on the server and is used for learning to read. Do not choose these modes for children’s games or where people who have not agreed appear.

Changes to this policy

When we change this policy, we rewrite this page and update the “Last updated” date at the top and bottom.
